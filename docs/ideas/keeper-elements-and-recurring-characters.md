# Keeper growth, earned elements, and recurring characters

Recorded 11 October 2026. These are new player design directions catalogued for future work, not implemented features. The immediate experiment is a standalone dive prototype. The existing domain/endgame notes are in [domain-and-endgame.md](domain-and-endgame.md).

## Keeper grows with the dragon

The Keeper already participates in some sleep decisions. Its apparent age should resemble the dragon's developmental stage. It should not remain a small child throughout a centuries-long campaign.

As the dragon matures, the Keeper develops into a grown, strong man with abilities. Available choices should change accordingly. Early options might use stealth, asking for help, tending injuries, or repairing minor protections; later options could include confronting intruders, commanding defenders, and using earned abilities. These examples are suggestions, not settled ability lists.

Use developmental stages rather than literal human age equal to dragon years. The appearance of an ancient dragon's Keeper remains open: elderly, ageless, or increasingly supernatural. Preserve the existing sleep-event format; this does not require a separate full RPG.

## Dragons do not start with fire

The player explicitly revises the earlier starting-fire assumption. Hatchlings begin with physical actions: flying, evading, snatching, biting, and clawing.

Fire or other abilities develop through actions and elemental attunement. Attunement should have meaningful requirements and may involve a special event, discovery, artifact, or combination of conditions. It should be something the player develops and pursues, rather than an automatic starting attack or purely cosmetic breed setting.

Open decisions: supported elements, prerequisites, how progress is revealed, the first unlock, whether multiple elements can be mastered, and whether any Keeper abilities share the dragon's attunement. Do not assume answers yet.

Fire, when acquired, is a broad mass-destruction technique. In overhead planning it should have limited precision, with a visible affected area and collateral consequences. It should not behave like a pinpoint attack on a bandit standing beside a farm. Physical attacks and careful grabs remain useful for precise objectives.

The standalone dive demo has no fire ability.

## Named characters who return and develop

The desired experience is similar to the memorable returning-character dynamic of Shadow of Mordor: encounters create individuals whose later development matters. Use original rules, characters, names, and presentation.

A named character may escape an attack at the last moment, remember what happened, and become determined to grow stronger. They could become:

- A powerful solo hero.
- A leader who recruits a hunting party or raises an army.
- A ruler who builds or takes over a kingdom.
- An ally, patron, supporter, or defender of the dragon.

Both hostile and friendly outcomes should occur. Escape from an attack is one origin, not the only one. Rescue, mercy, protection, betrayal, bargains, rivalry, and other events can all start or change a relationship.

Preserve identity, encounter history, motivation, and major changes across travel and sleep. Give the player recognizable consequences: a wounded captain who escaped now leads a trained company; someone the dragon rescued becomes a ruler who offers sanctuary.

Suggested guardrails to explore:

- Escapes should be readable and plausible, not arbitrary invulnerability on every finishing hit.
- Some fights must end conclusively.
- Rumors and the chronicle can announce major developments.
- Keep the active cast small enough to remember.
- Integrate with existing factions, settlements, armies, domains, and elapsed time.
- Account for mortal aging and succession across long sleeps.
- Relationships should evolve through multiple events rather than one permanent good/evil switch.

Open decisions include escape conditions, progression rate, recruiting or diplomacy, cast size, succession, and how supporters can change sides.

## Separate raid experiment

Build a standalone small playable test: overhead survey, select a chest, choose an approach, click Dive, steer through a perspective descent, dodge a clearly telegraphed archer, physically snatch treasure, escape, and retry quickly. Support touch, mouse, and keyboard.

This tests whether the flight and risk feel fun before integrating a new raid system. Keeper growth, attunement, and recurring characters remain catalogued ideas, not prototype dependencies.
